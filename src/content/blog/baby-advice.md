I've kept a running Notion page of parenting notes since our first was born, and I send it to every friend who tells me they're expecting. It made sense to put it somewhere easier to share. None of this is medical advice. It's just what worked in our house.

## Start here

- **Every baby is different.** Most parenting advice is something that worked well for one specific child, and results vary *heavily*. That's why you'll hear so much conflicting information.
- **Keep a reference book handy.** The *What to Expect* books (*When You're Expecting* and *The First Year*) were really nice to have. They help you sort out what's normal from what's worth worrying about.
- **Get term life insurance for both parents.** We went with 20–30 year terms and about $750,000 each. That's roughly what your partner and kids would need to live on if something happened to one of you. Pick a number that fits your own situation.

## Tips & tricks

- **Use sensitive wipes for everything.** They're mostly water. We use them after meals and for faces, hands, tables, everything. Regular wipes have alcohol, a distinct smell, and leave streaks and residue on surfaces.
- We use Pampers Sensitive wipes and Pampers Swaddlers diapers. Everyone has an opinion on this one.
- "You grow up with your first kid and raise the rest."
- "The days are long but the years are short."
- This Reddit thread of dad tips is worth reading: [r/daddit – Dad tips](https://www.reddit.com/r/daddit/comments/8uvq8o/dad_tips/)

## Hospital & newborn

- **Gurgling in the first day is (usually) amniotic fluid.** Your baby may sound like they're choking in the first day or so. The hospital should give you a small suction bulb to clear their mouth. Nobody warned us and we were terrified.
- **Watch the nurses swaddle.** They're amazing at it, so pay attention if you want to learn to swaddle with a cloth. If the hospital gives you a swaddle outfit as a gift, use it there until you get the hang of it.
- **Bring a Boppy** to the hospital if you're breastfeeding.
- **Use a baby tracking app for the first six months.** Track feedings, pees and poos, sleep, tummy time and so on. Baby Daybook worked well for our oldest, but it uses Google Assistant and our house runs on Alexa, so we switched to Hatch Baby.
- **Get out of the house once a day**, both mom and dad. Even a short walk helps a lot.
- **Skip the special baby detergent** unless your baby shows a skin reaction.
- **Always keep a hand on your baby on the changing table** if you aren't using the straps. Falls are terrifying.
- **Lay the clean diaper under the dirty one.** Clean up the baby, pull the dirty diaper out, and put the new one on. It keeps pees and poos from escaping onto the changing table, especially with very young babies.
- **Burrito the dirty diaper.** Roll it up with the used wipes inside and use the tabs to close it into a tidy little package.

## 3 months to 1 year

- **Trim nails while they're feeding.** They're calm, so it's easier and the clippers are less likely to slip.
- **Babies are tougher than they look.** Bumps, bruises, scrapes, cuts (even fairly big ones) and falls heal faster than you expect and are rarely as big a deal as they seem. They're really hard to mess up.
- **Rompers are elite.** Nothing to match and great diaper access. Long-pant rompers can be a pain to snap, though.
- **If they get stuck holding their breath mid-cry, blow on their face.** It usually resets them. Some babies can briefly pass out during a hard cry. Mention it to your pediatrician if it happens.
- **Avoid snap pajamas like the plague.** Get zipper or magnetic ones. With zippers, look for a double zipper (top and bottom) for easier changes.
- **Blowouts every day or two? Go up a diaper size.**
- **Double up crib sheets and mattress protectors.** If there's a middle-of-the-night mess (poopsplosion, throw-up, pee), pull the top layer off, deal with it in the morning, and put the baby back to bed.
- **Leaking overnight? Get nighttime diapers** or Pampers "Ultra" day-and-night diapers. They hold more at the cost of a little less freedom of movement.
- **Never go down a slide with a baby on your lap.** Their leg can catch and twist under your weight. [AAP-presented research](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/09/170915095226.htm) found fractures, usually of the lower leg, were the most common slide injury in kids under 6, and riding on a parent's lap raises the risk.
- **20-second hugs.**
  - They act as a reset when they're crying or screaming.
  - They build a stronger connection, which is especially important for dads.
  - You can count out loud to teach numbers when they're older.
- **Child cleaning triage:**
  1. Move the child out of reach of the mess.
  2. Clean the child to stop the spread: hands, then face, then clothes, then everything else.
  3. Set aside the pieces that can be cleaned later.
  4. Clean one item at a time.

## 1 to 4 years

- **Offer choices that both end where you want.** Kids always want more control over their lives. "Do you want to walk to your room, or do you want me to carry you?" gets them to bed and lets them feel in charge.
- **Turn repeat questions around.** "What's that?" "What do you think it is?"
- **Try swim lessons.** Some start as young as 3 months. It's a nice weekly activity and gets them some exercise.
- **Before baby #2 (or #3), spend time with your older kids.** They'll look so much older to you when you come home from the hospital. Our oldest seemed three years older the day we brought his brother home.
- **Get a red/green "OK to wake" light.** Red means nap or bedtime; green means it's OK to come out. Our rule is they don't have to sleep at nap time, but they do have to stay in their room while the light is red. We use a Hatch Rest, but other brands do the same thing.

## What to watch

**Skip Cocomelon at every age.** It's overstimulating and feels engineered to hook kids. Low-quality entertainment.

Streaming services below are where we've watched each show. They move around, so check before you subscribe to anything.

### Babies

- **[Ms. Rachel](https://www.youtube.com/@msrachel)** (YouTube): Fantastic for babies and toddlers just starting to talk. Some videos are made for 6 months and up, but most are aimed at 12 months and older. It was the only channel we let our boys watch at 18 months, the only thing that held our second's attention, and it kicked off a speech explosion.
- **Blippi**: Very annoying… but technically educational.

### Toddlers: less formulaic, lower stimulation

- **Bluey** (Disney+): Probably the best show to watch *with* your kids, toddler and up. One of our favorites.
- **Curious George** (Peacock): My favorite as a kid.
- **Pretzel and the Puppies** (Apple TV+): Based on books by the same creators as Curious George.
- **The Lion Guard** (Disney+): Fantastic songs, a plot that builds over three seasons, and good models of friendship and responsibility.
- **If You Give a Mouse a Cookie** (Prime Video): Based on the books.
- **Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood** (PBS): From the same company behind *Mister Rogers' Neighborhood*.
- **Dinosaur Train** (PBS and others): Older show with real dinosaur facts.
- **The Magic School Bus** (Netflix): A TV-cart-day classic.
- **Trash Truck** (Netflix)
- **Arthur** (PBS and others)
- **Sesame Street** (PBS and others): A classic.
- **Dragon Tales** (PBS, Prime Video)
- **Blue's Clues / Blue's Clues & You!**

### Toddlers: formulaic (and therefore annoying)

Stinky & Dirty, Paw Patrol, Rubble & Crew, Doc McStuffins, Peppa Pig, Mickey Mouse Clubhouse, Spidey and His Amazing Friends.

### Older kids

Avatar: The Last Airbender, Star Wars: The Clone Wars, Star Wars Resistance.

## Gear

There are tons of "everything you need" lists out there. Most are good enough and many overdo it. I'd start with the one in the *What to Expect* book. This isn't a complete list. It's the higher-impact stuff I developed strong opinions about through daily use.

- **Trade in old car seats at Target.** Target runs car seat trade-in events (a couple of times a year, I think) that give you a discount on new gear. Take advantage.
- **Skip the Diaper Genie.** It feels cheap, breaks easily, needs special bags, and smells just as much as an Ubbi or similar can.
- **Mirrors are all you need in the car.** Cameras add cables and a screen, and your baby can't see you.
- **Keekaroo Peanut changing pad.** It's wipeable, so it doesn't need a cover. Just clean it with a wipe.
- **Silicone bibs.** Nothing else is worth it. We travel with one and clean it with wipes.
- **Baby monitor:**
  - We use Arlo Baby over Wi-Fi, and I don't recommend it. It stops streaming after about 30 minutes if you're not on the same Wi-Fi, which makes overnight use frustrating.
  - I'd look for one with a dedicated handheld monitor you can carry around, ideally with an app too so you can check in when you're away or both parents can watch.
  - Lots of people use Google's Nest Cam. I've heard good things but haven't used it. If you already have Nest cameras, it's a compelling option.
- **Highchair:**
  - Avoid complicated seat covers with crevices where food gets stuck.
  - Look for built-in foam pads that wipe clean.
  - Skip Hop makes one in this style that we've liked.
- **Doona stroller/car seat.** A really cool design with no direct competitor, and the convenience during the infant stage is unmatched. Do your own research on its safety ratings. You'll need a separate stroller and car seat once your baby outgrows it, but not being locked into a system gives you more options later.
- **Otherwise, get a stroller/car seat system.** We got the Chicco KeyFit 30 combo. Many car seats and strollers can work across brands with a cheap adapter.
- **Buy three days' worth of whatever bottle your baby likes.** That lets you skip a day of dishes. We used Avent bottles for both boys.
- **Get the biggest Boon Lawn drying rack.** We still use ours for other things, months after the last bottle.
- **Skip the rectangular swaddle blanket.** Get one or two Halo sleep sacks or similar.
- **Magnetic cabinet locks.** Unlike most mechanical child locks, they don't let kids crack the door open, and you unlock them with a magnet. We use [these Vmaisi locks](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07C2PC3C1) and they work very well.
